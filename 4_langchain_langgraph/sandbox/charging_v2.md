# Vietnam public EV charging landscape

## Rough size of the public network
Public charging in Vietnam is led by VinFast/V-Green, and the market is already large enough for fleet use in major cities and along key corridors. A defensible rough estimate is **around 150,000 public charging points/ports nationwide**, though counts vary by source because some reports mix "charging points," "ports," and "stations." The clearest public figure surfaced in search is VinFast/V-Green's own claim of **more than 150,000 charging points** in Vietnam.

## Network 1: VinFast / V-Green
VinFast is the dominant network and is effectively the backbone of the market. Search results and the company's own materials indicate a network of **more than 150,000 charging points** across Vietnam. For fleet planning, that scale matters because it suggests the widest geographic coverage and the strongest chance of finding chargers outside the biggest cities. Caveat: the network is closely tied to VinFast's ecosystem, so fleets should confirm access rules, connector types, pricing, and whether their vehicles are supported before relying on it operationally.

## Network 2: EBOOST
EBOOST is the main non-VinFast network that appears fleet-relevant. Its official site describes itself as **"Trạm sạc xe điện EBOOST"** and positions the company as a **smart charging network across Vietnam**. Search results from EBOOST's site also indicate **more than 2,000** and in some snippets **more than 2,500 charging points nationwide**. That makes it a plausible second option for mixed-brand fleets, especially where open access matters more than absolute scale. As with any smaller network, confirm city coverage, uptime, payment methods, and vehicle compatibility before rollout.

## Fleet takeaway
For a company moving a sales fleet to EVs, Vietnam's public charging landscape appears usable but uneven: **VinFast/V-Green dominates scale**, while **EBOOST offers a smaller, more open alternative**. The practical question is less whether public charging exists and more whether the fleet's routes align with the strongest network coverage and whether vehicle compatibility is straightforward.
